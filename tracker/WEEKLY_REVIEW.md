# Weekly Review — 10 Minutes

This review is for learning and adjusting—not judging yourself. Use it once a week, or whenever you need a reset.

## Five questions

1. **What did I actually do?** List evidence, including small efforts.
2. **What helped me start?** A timer, a friend nearby, a prepared notebook, a quieter place, or a clear first step?
3. **What got in the way?** Phone scrolling, unclear tasks, fatigue, sleep, mood, or an unrealistic workload?
4. **What matters most next week?** Pick one academic priority, one build/clinical priority, and one maintenance priority.
5. **What will I make easier?** Remove one friction point and define the first action.

## Reset protocol

When I miss a day or lose momentum:
1. Stop trying to redesign my whole life.
2. Check basics: food, water, rest, and whether I need support.
3. Choose one task that takes five minutes or less.
4. Put the phone out of reach and start a short timer.
5. Resume from today. Do not double the workload to compensate.

## Minimum viable day

On a very difficult day, aim only for:
- [ ] Eat something and drink water.
- [ ] Attend essential commitments or contact someone if I cannot manage them.
- [ ] Open one subject and do five minutes of recall.
- [ ] Complete basic hygiene.
- [ ] Send one message or have one brief human interaction.

If even that is too much, prioritise immediate care and support. Productivity is not a measure of worth.

## Flexible study method

- **Choose:** one exact topic, not “study everything.”
- **Start:** 10 minutes; stopping at the timer is allowed.
- **Recall:** close notes and write/say three points.
- **Break:** stand, stretch, drink water.
- **Repeat:** only if useful and energy allows.
- **Body double:** study quietly with a classmate or on a call, if that helps.

## Four-week habit rollout

### Week 1 — Reduce starting friction
Choose only up to three supports:
- One realistic wake-up target or morning cue.
- One 10-minute study start on college days.
- Phone out of reach for that study block.

### Week 2 — Add retrieval practice
Keep the Week 1 supports. Add one short closed-book recall task after study.

### Week 3 — Make tomorrow easier
Keep what is working. Add a two-minute evening setup: bag ready and first task written down.

### Week 4 — Review and stabilise
Keep only habits that genuinely help. Add a weekly review; do not add more goals just because a new week started.

Change one thing at a time if the plan is overwhelming. If sleep or mood difficulties persist or worsen, discuss them with a qualified clinician; do not change prescribed medication without the prescriber's advice.
